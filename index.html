These are two different things. Sensitivity is how strongly a given amount affects you (receptor genetics, tolerance). How long it lasts is how fast your body clears it (mostly liver enzymes). You can be sensitive and still clear it fast.

How this works & sources

The model. Each drink is absorbed over roughly the first hour, then cleared at a steady rate set by your half-life (one-compartment, first-order absorption and elimination). With a 5-hour half-life, half of a dose is gone after about 5 hours and three quarters after about 10. The mg shown is caffeine remaining in your body. It is not a blood level.

Your half-life is a guess. In healthy adults it is often around 5 hours but ranges widely, from roughly 2 to 10 hours. Smoking shortens it. Pregnancy, oral contraceptives, liver disease, and some medications lengthen it, sometimes by a lot. Without lab testing, you can't know yours. If late caffeine hits you hard, try a longer half-life.

"OK at bed" is your choice. No study sets a safe bedtime level. The presets (25, 50, 100 mg) are rough starting points, not clinical cutoffs. Pick your own based on how you sleep.

L-theanine (in green tea and some preworkouts) may make caffeine feel smoother. It isn't modeled because it doesn't speed up how fast caffeine leaves your body.

What caffeine does. It works mainly by blocking adenosine receptors. Adenosine builds up while you're awake and drives sleepiness. Caffeine is not mainly a "dopamine boost".

Timing matters. In a small study (12 people), 400 mg taken even 6 hours before bed cut total sleep by over an hour, and people mostly didn't notice.

Daily amount. The FDA says up to about 400 mg a day is generally not linked to negative effects in healthy adults. ACOG advises under 200 mg a day during pregnancy.
